Three connected research directions

My research connects mathematical analysis, quantum physics, and the science of information. Three complementary directions examine the structures that make quantum information accessible, the physical processes that transform it, and its potential uses in computation, learning, and data analysis.

Mathematical foundations of quantum information

I study the geometric and algebraic structures underlying quantum states, measurements, and channels. My work connects frame theory, harmonic and functional analysis, operator algebras, and representation theory with tomography, state distinguishability, and information preservation. Questions from quantum information, in turn, motivate new problems in mathematical analysis.

Mathematical physics and quantum control

I investigate how dynamics, noise, and coherent interference shape access to quantum information. My work on fidelity preservation and interferometric coupling, together with ongoing research in dynamical tomography, connects mathematical analysis with open-system physics and motivates questions about quantum control and the realization of quantum processes.

Quantum computing, machine learning, and data science

Building on my work on quantum channels, error correction, and reconstruction, I aim to develop mathematical principles for reliable computation, learning, and inference. Developing directions include quantum data representations, quantum kernel learning, and estimation from noisy or incomplete measurements, connecting quantum information with the broader questions of data science.

Current research projects

My ongoing projects connect structural problems in analysis with physical measurement constraints, the resources needed to implement quantum processes, and the geometry of information protection.

Dynamical quantum tomography

I study how to identify an unknown pure quantum state by measuring repeated preparations at different times under known evolution, using a limited set of measurement outcomes. This project connects frame theory and observability with Lindblad dynamics and open quantum systems, examining how coherent evolution, dissipation, and sampling choices affect accessible information and reconstruction stability.

Related work: Phase retrievability of frames and quantum channels, Frame dimension functions and phase retrievability.

Channel structure and information preservation

I investigate the smallest intermediate system dimension needed to realize a quantum process in successive stages with prescribed noncommutative graph structures. Questions about a split property and protected subsystems connect operator theory and operator algebras with quantum error correction, information preservation, and the resources required to implement quantum processes.

Related work: Orbit-injective covariant quantum channels, Zero-error correctibility and phase retrievability for twirling channels.

Structured frames and quantum masking

I explore how a prescribed family of pure states can be encoded so that information is retained in correlations while each subsystem alone reveals no information about the encoded state. Gram matrices, frame operators, and constrained representations connect harmonic analysis with entanglement, state discrimination, and information protection. I also study how approximate masking affects information leakage and joint measurements.

Related work: Vanishing Moment Dilations and Applications to Balanced and Graph Frames, Extreme Points of Optimal Dual Frames for Erasures.
