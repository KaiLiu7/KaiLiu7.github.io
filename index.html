About me

Mathematics in quantum information, computing, and applications

I am a postdoctoral scholar at the University of Central Florida. My research explores the mathematical foundations of quantum information and quantum computing, connecting ideas from analysis and geometry with quantum physics and data science.

I investigate how the structure of quantum systems shapes what can be measured, reconstructed, and preserved. Connections among harmonic and functional analysis, operator algebras, frame theory, and group representations allow me to relate abstract mathematical questions to physical mechanisms such as interference and dissipation. This exchange between mathematics and physics guides my work on quantum measurements, dynamics, and information protection.

Building on these foundations, my broader research program extends toward quantum control, quantum machine learning, and mathematical data science, with an emphasis on reliable computation, learning, and inference under noise and limited measurements.

I received my Ph.D. in Mathematics from UCF in 2025 under the supervision of Deguang Han.

Research interests

  • Frame theory and phase retrieval; harmonic and functional analysis
  • Operator theory, operator algebras, and representation theory
  • Quantum tomography, measurement, and information preservation
  • Open quantum systems, quantum dynamics, and quantum control
  • Quantum algorithms, error correction, fault tolerance, and computational advantage
  • Quantum machine learning, machine learning, and mathematical data science

Research

What I work on

Research statement (PDF) ↗

My research connects mathematical analysis, quantum physics, and the science of information. Three complementary directions examine the structures that make quantum information accessible, the physical processes that transform it, and its potential uses in computation, learning, and data analysis.

01

Mathematical foundations of quantum information

I study the geometric and algebraic structures underlying quantum states, measurements, and channels. My work connects frame theory, harmonic and functional analysis, operator algebras, and representation theory with tomography, state distinguishability, and information preservation. Questions from quantum information, in turn, motivate new problems in mathematical analysis.

02

Mathematical physics and quantum control

I investigate how dynamics, noise, and coherent interference shape access to quantum information. My work on fidelity preservation and interferometric coupling, together with ongoing research in dynamical tomography, connects mathematical analysis with open-system physics and motivates questions about quantum control and the realization of quantum processes.

03

Quantum computing, machine learning, and data science

Building on my work on quantum channels, error correction, and reconstruction, I aim to develop mathematical principles for reliable computation, learning, and inference. Developing directions include quantum data representations, quantum kernel learning, and estimation from noisy or incomplete measurements, connecting quantum information with the broader questions of data science.
